FandomGobbler.

A browser extension for tracking fanfiction reading progress on Archive of Our Own and Fanfiction.net.

The Problem

Archive of Our Own has millions of stories across thousands of fandoms. In order to keep track of works, readers resort to browser bookmarks, spreadsheets, phone tabs, or just memory to categorize tens, hundreds, and yes, even thousands of works. Since there isn't a robust way to cleanly categorize and visualize these works, there's an ever present wish to have something that can sort through these works.

A solution that I worked towards is an in-browser tracker that lived alongside reading on AO3 without requiring a separate account or app.

What I Built

A sidebar-based web extension that lets you assign any AO3 story to one of six reading states (or creating their own), persisted locally by utilizing local storage. The sidebar is accessible from any page on AO3 via a floating button.

The six states mirror how readers actually think about their reading queues:

For Later Reading Completed Re-reading On Hold DNF

Available on Chrome, Firefox, Firefox for Android, and Edge with a shared codebase and browser-specific manifest variants.

Key Decisions

Why a sidebar? A popup closes the moment you click away which is not useful while reading. A sidebar stays open across page navigations within AO3, so you can check your list, update a status, and keep reading without losing your place.

Why six states? Most reading trackers offer three (want/reading/read), but people are constantly re-evaluating. People tend to re-read a work they really enjoyed, and just as equally put down a work they didn't care for. The ability to create your own "custom" category also encourages readers organize in a manner that suits their preference and needs.

Why local storage? In order to the most basic of organizing on AO3, a user would need an account, this extension is tied to local storage so there's no need for an account. It also keeps all data complete private to the user. The tradeoff is no sync across devices which is resolved by using Google Sheets as a database.

The Hardest Problem

AO3's DOM is inconsistent and there is no existing API. As a result any developer intending to do meaningful work is forced to create an internal database and almost appear bot-like in its engagement with the website. That and creating a dark/light mode on AO3 was like a dentist working on a root canal with no anesthesia (obligatory also not a dentist?).
